# Process 3 — Research Gap, Research Problem, Research Questions & Objectives

## Status
100% COMPLETE

## Research gap
The reviewed literature contains PMKVY-specific studies on programme impact, training effectiveness, implementation challenges and employment-related outcomes, while broader digital-government literature provides frameworks for data governance, data management and interoperability. However, within the literature reviewed for this study, there is limited integrated analysis of PMKVY audit evidence that brings together data quality, beneficiary verification, information retention, monitoring controls, system validation, Centre-State data integration and outcome tracking as interconnected governance functions.

## Research problem
PMKVY has relied on digital systems to support beneficiary registration, training, assessment, certification, monitoring and placement-related processes. However, CAG Report No. 20 of 2025 identified weaknesses in data quality, beneficiary eligibility verification, retention of electronic evidence, monitoring controls and Centre-State data integration. Existing PMKVY literature has examined programme impact, training effectiveness, implementation and employment-related outcomes, while broader digital-government research provides frameworks for data governance and interoperability. The research problem is therefore to examine the implications of these identified data and digital-governance weaknesses for the reliability of information used in beneficiary verification, programme monitoring, feedback and outcome tracking, and to identify evidence-based governance measures that can strengthen these functions.

## Main research question
What do the CAG audit findings on PMKVY reveal about weaknesses in data governance, beneficiary verification, digital monitoring, system validation, data integration and outcome tracking, and what evidence-based governance measures can strengthen these functions?

## Sub-research questions
1. What data-quality, data-retention and IT-control weaknesses were identified in the CAG audit of PMKVY, and what do these findings indicate about the reliability of programme information?
2. What weaknesses in beneficiary identity, age, educational qualification and eligibility verification were identified by the audit, and what are their implications for the reliability of beneficiary records?
3. What monitoring, system-validation and Centre-State data-integration issues were identified by the audit, and what implications do they have for programme oversight and feedback?
4. How do digital-governance measures introduced or proposed under PMKVY 4.0 and subsequent digital-system developments respond to the governance issues identified in the audit?

## Objectives
1. To examine the data-quality, data-retention and IT-control weaknesses identified in the CAG audit of PMKVY.
2. To analyse weaknesses in beneficiary identity and eligibility verification and their implications for the reliability of beneficiary records.
3. To examine monitoring, system-validation and Centre-State data-integration issues identified by the audit.
4. To examine relevant digital-governance measures introduced or proposed under PMKVY 4.0 and subsequent digital-system developments in relation to the audit findings.
5. To develop an evidence-based digital-governance framework for strengthening data reliability, beneficiary verification, monitoring, feedback and outcome tracking in large-scale government skill-development programmes.

## Significance
### Academic
Connects PMKVY research on training/implementation/outcomes with broader digital-government research on data governance and interoperability.

### Policy
Provides an evidence-based way to examine audit findings alongside subsequent digital-governance reforms rather than simply repeating the audit recommendations.

### Administrative
Connects data quality, verification, evidence retention, monitoring, interoperability, feedback and outcome tracking as related governance functions.

## Proposed contribution
The study's proposed contribution is an audit-based digital-governance perspective that systematically organises CAG's PMKVY findings across data quality, beneficiary verification, evidence retention, monitoring, system validation, interoperability and outcome tracking, and examines these findings against established digital-government governance concepts and subsequent PMKVY digital developments.

No “first study” or “unique study” claim is made.

## Scope
Included:
- PMKVY
- PMKVY 1.0–3.0 audit evidence
- 2015–2022 main audit period
- relevant PMKVY 4.0/SIDH developments
- data quality
- beneficiary verification
- IT controls
- monitoring
- system validation
- Centre-State integration
- feedback
- outcome tracking

Not central:
- general Indian cybersecurity
- AI/blockchain for their own sake
- software development
- complete PMKVY economic impact evaluation
- general unemployment analysis
- political evaluation

## Boundaries
- Audit observations from selected states must not automatically be generalised to all India.
- Audit findings do not by themselves prove fraud, corruption, intentional misconduct or cyberattack.
- Data-quality weaknesses do not equal fake beneficiaries.
- Placement statistics must not be presented as caused by digital-governance weaknesses without causal evidence.
- Later PMKVY 4.0/SIDH controls must be separated from the 2015–2022 audit period.
