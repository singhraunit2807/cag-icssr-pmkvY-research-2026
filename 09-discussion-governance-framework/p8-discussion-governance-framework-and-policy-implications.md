# Process 8 — Evidence-Based Discussion, Governance Framework & Policy Implications

## Status
Complete.

## Purpose

This process interprets the verified P7 findings, connects them with the P5 analytical framework and P6 reform trajectory, and develops the study's evidence-based governance framework and policy implications.

The discussion does not rank political actors, programmes or reforms. It does not claim that PMKVY failed or that later reforms succeeded. It distinguishes:

- audit evidence;
- researcher interpretation;
- official reform/design evidence;
- broader governance principles.

## 1. Central discussion finding

The CAG evidence is most coherently interpreted as a **governance reliability problem across an interconnected digital information chain**, rather than simply as a technology-availability problem.

The chain is:

**Digital programme system → data generation → data quality → identity/eligibility verification → evidence retention and traceability → monitoring/system validation → Centre-State information access → feedback → outcome tracking → programme oversight and course correction**

The significance of the audit is that weaknesses appeared at multiple points in this chain. A weakness at one stage can reduce the reliability or usefulness of information at later stages.

This is a researcher-developed synthesis based on the CAG findings. It is not presented as a CAG quotation.

## 2. Discussion Theme 1 — Digitisation does not automatically create reliable administrative information

The audit identified substantial data-quality and traceability problems, including missing/invalid bank-account fields, repeated account identifiers, incomplete trainer/assessor information and contact-data problems.

The analytical implication is not that the digital platform was useless. Rather, **digitisation and data reliability are separate governance requirements**.

A digital system can store large quantities of records while still producing information that is incomplete, inconsistent, difficult to validate or insufficiently traceable.

This interpretation is consistent with the OECD's 2026 Digital Government Outlook, which distinguishes the availability of digital foundations from their effective operational use. OECD notes that data-governance strategies and infrastructure are increasingly common, while data-quality management, implementation and impact measurement can lag. citeturn0search0turn0search1

## 3. Discussion Theme 2 — Verification must be treated as a control, not merely a field

The CAG's findings on age and educational eligibility demonstrate why simply having demographic or qualification fields in a registration system is insufficient.

For a field to function as a governance control, the system needs:

1. a defined requirement;
2. a valid data source;
3. a verification rule;
4. an exception pathway;
5. an auditable decision;
6. periodic validation.

Therefore:

**Field availability ≠ verification control.**

This is particularly relevant to the CAG's observation that the system did not have adequate mechanisms to verify age/DOB against job-role requirements.

The Qualification Pack limitation must remain explicit: CAG's comparison involved the available/latest QP Master File because the version actually used for training was not consistently captured. Therefore, the research interprets the findings as evidence of weaknesses in the verification architecture, without converting every flagged record into a definitive intentional eligibility violation.

## 4. Discussion Theme 3 — Evidence retention is part of accountability

The audit's findings concerning retention of electronic evidence are important because monitoring cannot be independently reconstructed if essential source records are not retained.

A monitoring system therefore requires:

**record creation → secure retention → traceability → validation → retrieval → review → corrective action**

The policy implication is that a data-retention policy should not be treated as an IT-storage issue alone. It is an accountability mechanism.

This aligns with CAG Recommendation 4, which called for strict enforcement of PMKVY 4.0 IT controls and a data-retention policy specifying what data should be retained, where it should be retained, for how long and by whom.

## 5. Discussion Theme 4 — Monitoring quality depends on validation, not only inspection volume

The audit examined 1,463 inspection reports and identified repeated photographs, same-day multi-location reporting and other validation weaknesses.

The analytical lesson is:

**More inspections ≠ stronger monitoring.**

Monitoring becomes stronger when evidence can be independently validated and exceptions trigger action.

A robust monitoring control should therefore measure:

- completeness of inspection evidence;
- location/time consistency;
- identity of the inspector;
- duplicate-evidence detection;
- exception detection;
- corrective action;
- closure of corrective action.

PMKVY 4.0's monitoring architecture includes monthly progress reporting, internal monitoring arrangements and a penalty matrix. These are design/implementation responses, not by themselves proof of effectiveness. citeturn0search42

## 6. Discussion Theme 5 — Interoperability must be judged by usability

CAG reported that in six of the eight audited States, SSDMs lacked access to central-component training data from NSDC.

The research therefore distinguishes:

**Technical connectivity → data availability → authorised access → usable information → administrative action**

Only the last stages demonstrate governance usefulness.

The OECD's 2026 Digital Government Outlook similarly argues that interoperability is not merely a technical connection. Systems need governance arrangements, common standards, clear responsibilities and sustained operational use; the existence of an interoperability system alone does not guarantee impact. citeturn0search0

This directly supports the study's distinction between **interoperability by design** and **interoperability in administrative practice**.

## 7. Discussion Theme 6 — Feedback is a governance loop

The CAG found very limited candidate feedback and serious contact-data limitations in the beneficiary survey.

The significance is not merely that the programme collected “too little feedback.” The deeper issue is whether the feedback mechanism forms a functioning loop:

**candidate experience → feedback capture → reliable aggregation → analysis → responsible authority → corrective action → subsequent monitoring**

Without the final stages, feedback becomes information collection rather than governance.

PMKVY's monitoring framework has explicitly recognised candidate feedback as a mechanism intended to improve scheme delivery, including IVR/OBD-based post-certification feedback. citeturn0search43

## 8. Discussion Theme 7 — Outcome tracking must go beyond certification

Certification is an implementation output. It does not by itself establish whether training generated sustained labour-market value.

The CAG's placement figures are therefore useful as programme-performance information but should not be treated as evidence of a causal digital-governance effect.

The broader governance implication is:

**enrolment → training → assessment → certification → placement → employment retention/income → longer-term labour-market trajectory**

The further the system tracks along this chain, the more useful the information becomes for programme evaluation and course correction.

The OECD similarly identifies impact measurement and outcome use as an important part of moving from digital foundations to meaningful public-sector results. citeturn0search0turn0search5

## 9. Integrated Governance Framework

The study's proposed framework is:

### Control Layer 1 — Data Reliability

Questions:
- Is the data field complete?
- Is the value valid?
- Is duplication detected?
- Is the source identifiable?
- Is historical information traceable?
- Are retention rules defined?

### Control Layer 2 — Verification Reliability

Questions:
- Is identity verified?
- Is age verified against authoritative information?
- Is qualification verified against the applicable job-role requirement?
- Are duplicate/exception cases resolved?
- Is the verification decision auditable?

### Control Layer 3 — Evidence Reliability

Questions:
- Is monitoring evidence time/location identifiable?
- Can the evidence be independently validated?
- Are duplicate or inconsistent records automatically flagged?
- Are exceptions escalated?

### Control Layer 4 — Interoperability & Information Access

Questions:
- Can authorised Centre and State actors access required data?
- Is the information complete and timely?
- Are common data standards used?
- Can information move across systems without unnecessary duplication?

### Control Layer 5 — Feedback & Outcome Tracking

Questions:
- Can beneficiaries reliably provide feedback?
- Are responses representative enough for interpretation?
- Is post-certification information captured?
- Are placement/employment outcomes followed over time?

### Control Layer 6 — Governance Use

Questions:
- Who reviews the information?
- What thresholds trigger action?
- Is corrective action recorded?
- Is closure verified?
- Does evidence change programme implementation?

## 10. Five-control test

Every major PMKVY digital control can be tested using five questions:

1. **Existence:** Does the control exist?
2. **Operation:** Does it operate?
3. **Validation:** Is its output independently checked?
4. **Usability:** Can authorised managers use the information?
5. **Course correction:** Does the information lead to documented action?

This five-control test converts the research from a simple audit-summary exercise into a governance analysis.

## 11. Reform maturity matrix

Later PMKVY 4.0/SIDH reforms should be classified as:

| Evidence level | Meaning | Example type |
|---|---|---|
| A — Design | Control is specified | Guideline requirement |
| B — Implementation | Deployment/use is reported | Government implementation report |
| C — Operational | Performance data show consistent operation | Error/exception/coverage evidence |
| D — Effectiveness | Credible evidence shows improved outcomes/control reliability | Independent or robust evaluation |

The current evidence base supports substantial **A-level** reform evidence and some **B-level** implementation/enforcement evidence. It should not automatically be upgraded to C or D.

## 12. Indian governance-framework connection

NITI Aayog's DGQI work provides a useful Indian public-sector conceptual reference. Its framework covers data strategy, systems and data-driven outcomes, with themes including data generation, data quality, technology integration, analysis, dissemination and security. citeturn0search41

For this study, DGQI is used as a **conceptual comparison framework**, not as evidence that PMKVY complies or fails to comply with DGQI unless a direct scheme-level assessment is available.

The PMKVY findings can therefore be read through three broad DGQI-aligned concerns:

**Data generation/quality → technology and integration → data-driven outcomes**

The research adds a more detailed programme-control chain by inserting beneficiary verification, evidence retention, monitoring validation and feedback between data generation and outcome use.

## 13. Policy implications

### 13.1 Introduce control-level data-quality indicators

PMKVY dashboards should not rely only on aggregate participation and certification counts. Governance dashboards can track:

- missing mandatory fields;
- invalid values;
- duplicate identities;
- unresolved exceptions;
- stale records;
- evidence-retention completeness.

### 13.2 Make eligibility verification rule-based

For each job role, the system should maintain:

- applicable qualification version;
- minimum age;
- educational requirement;
- prior technical qualification, where applicable;
- work-experience requirement, where applicable;
- authoritative verification source;
- exception-resolution status.

### 13.3 Create an evidence-retention register

For every critical digital process, specify:

**what is retained → where → retention period → responsible authority → access rights → audit trail**

### 13.4 Measure monitoring controls through exceptions

Instead of only counting inspections, monitor:

**inspection → validation → exception → action → closure**

This makes monitoring performance measurable as a control process.

### 13.5 Build practical Centre-State data access

Integration should be assessed through:

- data completeness;
- timeliness;
- authorised access;
- usability;
- system uptime;
- unresolved data mismatches;
- documented use by State/District authorities.

### 13.6 Close the feedback loop

Feedback systems should report:

- contactability;
- response rate;
- response quality;
- representativeness limitations;
- issue categories;
- action taken;
- closure rate.

### 13.7 Track outcomes longitudinally

Post-certification tracking should progressively examine:

- placement;
- employment status;
- job retention;
- income change;
- relevance of skill to employment;
- transition to further training/entrepreneurship.

These indicators should be treated as outcome evidence only when their definitions, populations and collection methods are clearly documented.

## 14. What the framework does not claim

The framework does **not** claim:

- that PMKVY is unsuccessful overall;
- that every anomalous record represents fraud;
- that every monitoring anomaly represents deliberate fabrication;
- that digital controls cause employment outcomes;
- that PMKVY 4.0/SIDH have eliminated the historical weaknesses;
- that technical interoperability automatically creates effective Centre-State governance.

## 15. Discussion conclusion

The study's central analytical conclusion is:

> **The governance value of a digital skill-development system depends not simply on the existence of digital infrastructure, but on whether information remains reliable, verifiable, traceable, interoperable, usable for oversight and connected to feedback and outcome-based course correction.**

The CAG evidence provides the empirical basis for identifying weaknesses across this chain. PMKVY 4.0 and SIDH provide a later reform layer containing several controls that correspond to those weaknesses. The research contribution lies in connecting these two layers through an evidence-maturity framework rather than treating the existence of a new digital mechanism as proof of successful reform.
