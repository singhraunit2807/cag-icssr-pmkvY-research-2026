## Research design

This is an audit-based secondary/documentary study using:

1. qualitative document analysis;
2. descriptive quantitative analysis of verified audit figures; and
3. comparative policy/document analysis of later PMKVY 4.0/SIDH developments.

The design is descriptive and analytical, not causal.

## Primary empirical source

The primary empirical source is CAG Report No. 20 of 2025, *Skill Development under Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana*.

The CAG report states that its Performance Audit covers the first three phases of PMKVY launched during 2015–22 and was conducted at MSDE, NSDC and eight selected States: Assam, Bihar, Jharkhand, Kerala, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh. The report also examines later responses/updates, including PMKVY 4.0 information. These periods and scopes will be kept separate in the article. citeturn4view1

## Unit of analysis

The unit of analysis is a **documented PMKVY governance/audit observation and its associated governance function/control**, not an individual beneficiary.

Individual candidate figures are therefore treated as evidence within an audit observation, not as individually sampled research subjects.

## Analytical variables/dimensions

Because this is not a causal statistical study, “variables” are operational analytical dimensions rather than independent/dependent variables:

1. **Data reliability:** completeness, validity, duplication, consistency and traceability.
2. **Beneficiary verification:** identity, age, education, technical qualification and eligibility.
3. **Evidence retention:** retention policy, electronic records, photographs/videos and audit trails.
4. **Monitoring/control validation:** inspection evidence, attendance systems, geo-location, identity/time checks and exception validation.
5. **Interoperability/information access:** Centre-State data exchange, API/data consistency and administrative usability.
6. **Feedback/outcome tracking:** beneficiary feedback, placement information and post-certification tracking.
7. **Governance use:** whether information is available for oversight, corrective action and course correction.

## Analytical framework

**Digital programme system**
→ **Data generation**
→ **Data reliability**
→ **Identity/eligibility verification**
→ **Evidence retention & traceability**
→ **Monitoring & system validation**
→ **Centre-State information access**
→ **Feedback**
→ **Outcome tracking**
→ **Programme oversight & course correction**

This framework is a researcher-developed synthesis grounded in the CAG audit objectives/findings and the verified literature. It is not presented as a CAG quotation.

## Evidence-analysis procedure

For each major finding:

1. identify the exact CAG finding;
2. record the exact numerical/documentary evidence;
3. record period, population and geographic scope;
4. assign the governance dimension;
5. record what the source itself establishes;
6. identify the documented or logically bounded governance implication;
7. compare with a relevant governance principle/framework where useful;
8. identify a corresponding control requirement;
9. identify later official reform evidence, if relevant;
10. classify the later reform by evidence maturity;
11. record remaining uncertainty or limitation.

Core chain:

**Claim → Source → Exact Evidence → Period → Population/Scope → Interpretation**

## Quantitative analysis

Only descriptive calculations from verified CAG figures will be used.

For example:

**Percentage = (Relevant records / Total records) × 100**

Where CAG already reports the percentage, the CAG percentage will normally be retained rather than unnecessarily recalculated.

No regression, causal inference, predictive modelling or individual-level statistical estimation is planned. The available evidence and research questions do not support such causal estimation.

## Qualitative analysis

CAG observations will be coded thematically under:

- Data Reliability
- Beneficiary Verification
- Evidence Retention
- Monitoring
- System Validation
- Interoperability/Integration
- Feedback
- Outcome Tracking
- Governance Use

## Comparative policy/document analysis

Historical audit evidence from PMKVY 1.0–3.0 will be compared with subsequent PMKVY 4.0/SIDH mechanisms.

A later mechanism will be classified as:

- **A — Design evidence**
- **B — Implementation evidence**
- **C — Operational evidence**
- **D — Effectiveness evidence**

The existence of a guideline, platform or announced mechanism will not be treated as proof of effectiveness.

## Tools employed

The methodology uses:

- structured evidence matrix;
- spreadsheet calculations and denominator checks;
- thematic coding matrix;
- source-verification log;
- reference-management/citation-control process for MLA 9;
- document comparison of CAG and official reform documents.

No specialised software or technical tool will be claimed in the article unless it was actually used and materially contributed to the analysis.

## Validity and reliability

Reliability will be supported through:

- primary-source prioritisation;
- exact-figure verification;
- denominator and scope checks;
- separation of audit and reform periods;
- triangulation with official and academic sources;
- explicit distinction between source-derived facts, researcher interpretation and proposed policy measures.

## Limitations

- The study relies primarily on documentary/secondary evidence.
- CAG findings have a defined audit scope, including an eight-State selected component.
- The principal audit period is 2015–22, while later PMKVY 4.0/SIDH evidence belongs to a subsequent period.
- The study does not establish causal relationships between digital-governance weaknesses and employment/placement outcomes.
- Later reform effectiveness cannot be assessed beyond the evidence actually available.
- Qualification/eligibility findings must retain CAG's stated limitation concerning the version of Qualification Packs available for comparison.

## Causality boundary

The study may identify a documented control weakness, classify it, explain its implications for information reliability/oversight, and compare it with governance principles or later controls.

The study will not infer fraud, corruption, intentional manipulation, cyberattack, fake beneficiaries, or causal employment effects without direct supporting evidence.
