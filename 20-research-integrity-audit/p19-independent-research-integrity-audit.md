## 3. CAG numerical integrity checks

### Data reliability
- 90,66,264 of 95,90,801 PMKVY 2.0/3.0 participant records with zero/Null/N/A/blank bank-account detail = 94.53%.
- 12,122 unique bank accounts repeated for 52,381 participants.
- These are correctly presented as information-control concerns, not proof of fraud.

### Eligibility
- 6.54 lakh candidates across 705 job roles below minimum entry age according to the latest QP Master File used by CAG.
- The article retains the critical QP-version limitation for PMKVY 1.0 and about 60.95 lakh PMKVY 2.0/3.0 certified candidates.
- PMKVY 4.0 updated-data finding: 1.18 lakh underage candidates across 365 job roles as of 9 October 2024.
- Education: 6,77,807 cases with required information not recorded and 8,09,046 candidates not fulfilling minimum educational qualifications.
- Work experience: system field existed but compliance mechanism was not brought into effect.

### Assessment and monitoring
- Assessment delays up to 1,270 days.
- Certification delays beyond seven days in 82,158 batches involving 23.87 lakh candidates.
- 1,463 inspection reports scrutinised.
- 45 monitoring reports used the same photograph as evidence for different trainings.
- 80 same-day multi-State inspection instances involving the same NSDC inspector(s).
- 1,232 cases, or 84.21%, lacked geo-tagging.
- 295 cases had less than five minutes between initiation of inspections by the same inspector on the same date at different locations.
- AEBAS compliance: 12,538 of 98,488 PMKVY 2.0/3.0 STT/SP batches, covering 3,09,615 of 23,64,359 participants, about 13%.

### Centre-State and feedback
- Six of eight audited States lacked access to central-component training data from NSDC.
- 73,100 trainees, less than one per cent of certified candidates, had submitted feedback as of September 2022.

### Outcomes
- 56.14 lakh certified and 23.18 lakh placed under STT/SP.
- Overall placement rate 41.29%.
- Phase rates: PMKVY 1.0 = 16.74%; PMKVY 2.0 = 51.08%; PMKVY 3.0 = 13.47%.
- Article explicitly treats these as descriptive and not causal evidence of digital-governance effects.
