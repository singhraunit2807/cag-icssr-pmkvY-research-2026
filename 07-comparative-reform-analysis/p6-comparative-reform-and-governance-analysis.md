# Process 6 — Comparative Reform & Governance Analysis

## Status
Complete.

## Purpose

This process compares governance weaknesses documented in CAG Report No. 20 of 2025 with subsequent PMKVY 4.0 and Skill India Digital Hub (SIDH) design and implementation measures. The comparison is analytical, not evaluative ranking. A later mechanism is treated as evidence of a proposed or implemented control, not as proof that the underlying governance problem has been solved.

## Evidence boundary

- CAG audit evidence primarily concerns PMKVY 1.0–3.0 and the period up to March 2022, with subsequent replies/updates examined in 2024.
- PMKVY 4.0 was introduced as a later phase and must be analysed separately.
- SIDH was launched on 13 September 2023 as a unified digital platform for the skilling ecosystem.
- Current government-reported figures and corrective measures are used only as evidence of reported implementation/design, not independent evidence of effectiveness.

## Key reform findings

### 1. From platform availability to control architecture

The CAG audit did not identify the absence of digitisation as the central problem. Instead, it documented weaknesses in the reliability and governance of information generated through the digital implementation process. The later PMKVY 4.0 guidelines respond by specifying multiple controls across registration, attendance, monitoring, verification, feedback and post-certification stages. The analytical shift is therefore from asking whether a digital platform exists to asking whether controls operate throughout the information lifecycle.

### 2. Identity verification is being moved closer to registration

PMKVY 4.0 guidelines require mobile-based OTP/e-KYC profile verification and de-duplication, while candidate profiling can use information from other government portals. Later government reporting also describes Aadhaar-based authentication and face authentication. This is relevant to the CAG findings on unreliable contact information and weak beneficiary verification.

However, the research must not state that these measures have eliminated inaccurate records. The appropriate conclusion is that the later design contains stronger identity and authentication controls; operational effectiveness requires separate evidence.

### 3. Monitoring has become more multi-layered

The PMKVY 4.0 monitoring framework combines biometric attendance, CCTV-based monitoring, virtual verification and call validation. It also provides for escalation where attendance deviations require further validation. This directly addresses the weakness identified by CAG that inspection evidence could be difficult to validate and that system-validation checks needed strengthening.

The research implication is important: a monitoring control should be assessed through its full chain — signal generation, validation, exception handling, action and feedback — rather than by the mere presence of an application or dashboard.

### 4. Interoperability is broader, but Centre-State usability remains a distinct question

SIDH is officially described as an interoperable digital public infrastructure connecting skilling services and other government platforms. This is relevant to the CAG recommendation for convergence and data integration.

Nevertheless, interoperability between central platforms and external government systems is not identical to effective Centre-State programme oversight. The study should separately ask whether State authorities receive the relevant data, whether the data are timely and complete, and whether they can use the information for monitoring and course correction.

### 5. Outcome tracking is becoming more explicit

PMKVY 4.0 incorporates post-certification tracking and, for applicable courses, On-the-Job Training. These design elements create a pathway from training participation to post-training information.

This is consistent with the broader data-governance principle that administrative data should support not only implementation monitoring but also outcome measurement and policy correction. NITI Aayog's DGQI framework similarly emphasises data strategy, data systems and data-driven outcomes, while noting the importance of data quality, technology integration and outcome-level data. ### 6. Enforcement evidence is stronger than effectiveness evidence

Government reporting in July 2026 states that PMKVY 4.0 includes Aadhaar-based e-KYC, face authentication, geo-tagged attendance, QR-coded certificates, real-time SIDH dashboards, strengthened accreditation, independent monitoring, revised KPIs and penalty/recovery mechanisms. The same reporting states that more than ₹400 crore was withheld for further scrutiny due to trainer-attendance non-compliance during FY 2023–24 to FY 2025–26. These are evidence of reported controls and enforcement actions, not independent proof that the programme's governance problems have been fully resolved. citeturn1search0

## Reform-to-control matrix

The comparative analysis can be organised as follows:

**CAG weakness → Required governance control → PMKVY 4.0/SIDH response → Evidence needed for effectiveness**

1. Incomplete/weak data → retention, ownership, quality and traceability controls → digital lifecycle and platform-based records → retention completeness, audit trails and exception rates.
2. Weak beneficiary verification → identity, de-duplication and eligibility checks → OTP/e-KYC, Aadhaar/face authentication, profiling and de-duplication → verified-record accuracy and exclusion of ineligible/duplicate records.
3. Weak monitoring evidence → independent, time/location-verifiable monitoring → AEBAS, geo-tagging, CCTV, virtual verification and call validation → false-positive/false-negative rates, exception resolution and action taken.
4. Weak Centre-State integration → interoperable and accessible data exchange → SIDH interoperability and platform integration → completeness, timeliness and usability of State-level data.
5. Weak feedback/outcome tracking → regular feedback and post-certification information → call validation, feedback and post-certification tracking → response quality, longitudinal outcome coverage and use in course correction.
6. Weak accountability → measurable KPIs and consequences → monitoring guidelines, penalties, recoveries and payment scrutiny → consistency of enforcement and reduction in repeated non-compliance.

## Important analytical distinction

The CAG recommendations themselves provide a useful bridge between the audit and later reform analysis. In particular, Recommendation 3 calls for Centre-State mapping and data integration; Recommendation 4 calls for strict enforcement of PMKVY 4.0 IT controls and a data-retention policy; Recommendation 5 concerns UDISE integration and minimum entry criteria; Recommendation 7 concerns system-validation checks; and Recommendation 9 concerns regular monitoring and feedback for evaluation and course correction. citeturn1search14

The comparison should therefore not be framed as “CAG found problems and the government later fixed them.” A more defensible formulation is:

> The audit identified specific weaknesses in the information and control chain, while subsequent PMKVY 4.0 and SIDH designs introduced or strengthened several controls that correspond to those weaknesses. The available evidence is sufficient to analyse the reform trajectory, but not to conclude that all identified weaknesses have been eliminated.

## Evidence maturity model for later reforms

For every PMKVY 4.0/SIDH reform, use four evidence levels:

- **A — Design evidence:** guideline, policy, system specification or announced mechanism exists.
- **B — Implementation evidence:** government or administrative evidence shows that the mechanism has been deployed or used.
- **C — Operational evidence:** data demonstrate that the mechanism functions consistently and produces usable control information.
- **D — Effectiveness evidence:** credible evidence demonstrates improved data reliability, verification, monitoring, feedback or outcomes attributable to the mechanism.

Current verified sources provide substantial A-level evidence and some B-level/enforcement evidence. They should not automatically be upgraded to C or D.

## Research implications

The comparative analysis produces four implications for the final article:

1. **Digitisation should be analysed as a governance chain, not as a technology intervention alone.**
2. **Control effectiveness should be assessed separately from control existence.**
3. **Interoperability should be examined for actual administrative usability, not merely technical connectivity.**
4. **Outcome tracking should extend beyond enrolment and certification toward post-training trajectories, while avoiding unsupported causal claims.**

## What should NOT be claimed

- Do not claim PMKVY 4.0 has solved the CAG-identified problems.
- Do not claim SIDH interoperability proves effective Centre-State integration.
- Do not claim authentication mechanisms prove absence of duplicate/fake beneficiaries.
- Do not claim withheld payments prove widespread wrongdoing.
- Do not claim new monitoring technology automatically improves placement or employment outcomes.
- Do not infer causal impact of digital reforms on employment without appropriate evidence.
