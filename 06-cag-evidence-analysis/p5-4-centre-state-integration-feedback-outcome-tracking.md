# P5.4 — Centre-State Data Integration, Feedback & Outcome Tracking Analysis

## Status
COMPLETE

## Scope
This sub-process analyses:
1. Centre-State data integration and access;
2. beneficiary feedback;
3. placement/outcome tracking;
4. later PMKVY 4.0/SIDH mechanisms relevant to outcome tracking.

The main audit evidence relates to PMKVY 1.0–3.0 and the selected-state audit. Later PMKVY 4.0/SIDH evidence is treated as a separate reform period.

## Analytical findings

### Finding 1 — Interoperability is a governance control
The Centre-State issue is not simply a technical API problem. If a State monitoring body cannot access relevant central-component data, its ability to perform oversight is constrained.

Therefore:

**Data generated at Centre → Data transferred/integrated → State access → State monitoring → Corrective action**

A break in this chain weakens decentralised oversight.

### Finding 2 — Data integration must be judged by usability, not connection alone
An API or interoperable platform can exist while data remain delayed, mismatched or inaccessible. Therefore, the study should evaluate interoperability through:
- completeness,
- consistency,
- timeliness,
- accessibility,
- traceability,
- use in monitoring decisions.

### Finding 3 — Feedback is an information-to-correction mechanism
The beneficiary feedback findings connect data quality with programme learning. If contact information is unreliable or participation is very low, the feedback loop becomes weak.

The relevant chain is:

**Beneficiary → Feedback → Analysis → Corrective action → Programme improvement**

### Finding 4 — Outcome tracking must go beyond certification
The CAG placement figures demonstrate that certification and placement are different indicators. PMKVY 4.0's one-year post-certification tracking design is therefore relevant because it attempts to observe career progression after training.

However:

**Tracking mechanism exists ≠ tracking data are reliable ≠ outcomes improved.**

These three propositions must remain separate throughout the article.

### Finding 5 — Placement data cannot be used to establish a digital-governance causal effect
The placement rates differ substantially across PMKVY phases. But placement outcomes can be influenced by job-role choice, labour-market demand, candidate characteristics, local economic conditions, employer participation, training quality and other factors.

Therefore the research will use placement data as **context and an outcome indicator**, not as proof that data-governance weaknesses caused employment outcomes.

### Finding 6 — Later PMKVY 4.0/SIDH architecture provides a reform comparator
PMKVY 4.0 introduces:
- e-KYC/Aadhaar-based authentication,
- candidate de-duplication,
- profile integration,
- candidate feedback,
- physical inspection monitoring,
- post-certification tracking.

SIDH later provides a broader interoperable platform linking training, certification, employment and entrepreneurship services.

This allows the research to compare:

**Audit-identified control weakness → subsequent control design → evidence of implementation → evidence of effectiveness**

The final step should not be assumed.

## Research-safe interpretation

> CAG evidence indicates that fragmented Centre-State information flows and weak feedback mechanisms constrained monitoring and programme learning, while placement data demonstrate the need to distinguish certification from employment outcomes. PMKVY 4.0 and SIDH subsequently introduced or expanded mechanisms for interoperability, candidate verification, feedback and post-certification tracking. These later mechanisms can be assessed as governance responses, but their existence alone does not establish effectiveness or causal improvement in employment outcomes.

## Current-context evidence boundary

Official 2026 government sources describe SIDH as an interoperable platform and report large-scale registrations and PMKVY 4.0 digital implementation. These are useful for documenting the **current architecture and stated reforms**. They should not be presented as independent proof that the earlier audit weaknesses have been fully resolved.

The 2026 government-reported PMKVY 4.0 evaluation is potentially valuable for the outcome section, but before using its numerical results as a major finding, the full evaluation report and methodology should be examined. The current government summary alone is insufficient for a detailed causal interpretation.
