## The Five Control Questions

The integrated analysis should test every digital governance mechanism through five questions:

### 1. Does the control exist?
Is there a documented rule, field, system function, policy or procedure?

### 2. Does the control operate?
Is the mechanism actually used during implementation?

### 3. Is the output validated?
Does the system or responsible authority check whether the recorded information is complete, valid, unique, timely and attributable?

### 4. Is the information usable for oversight?
Can authorised monitoring bodies access and interpret the information needed for their responsibilities?

### 5. Is the information used for course correction?
Is there evidence that feedback, monitoring or outcome information informs administrative action or programme improvement?

This five-question model is a researcher-developed analytical tool. It should be labelled as such in the final article.

## Core Research Insight

The CAG findings are better understood as interconnected control weaknesses rather than as isolated database errors.

A simplified causal-logic chain for governance analysis is:

**Weak Data Capture**
→ **Unreliable Administrative Record**
→ **Weak Verification / Traceability**
→ **Less Reliable Monitoring Evidence**
→ **Constrained Feedback / Oversight**
→ **Weaker Evidence for Course Correction**

This is an analytical logic chain, not a statistical causal model. The study must not claim that CAG evidence proves a causal effect on employment outcomes.

## Three Levels of Digital Governance Reliability

### Level 1 — Data Reliability
Can the system produce complete, valid, unique, traceable and retained records?

Relevant CAG evidence:
- null/repeated/invalid data;
- missing records;
- weak retention;
- contact-data problems.

### Level 2 — Control Reliability
Can the system use those records to enforce or validate rules?

Relevant CAG evidence:
- age/education/work-experience verification;
- AEBAS;
- geo-tagging;
- shared logins;
- inspection validation.

### Level 3 — Governance Usefulness
Can the information support oversight, feedback, outcome tracking and course correction?

Relevant CAG evidence:
- State access to central data;
- beneficiary feedback;
- placement/outcome tracking;
- post-certification tracking requirements.

This three-level structure helps prevent the article from treating all IT problems as equivalent.

## Important distinction: design, implementation and effectiveness

For every later reform, use the following classification:

**A. Design evidence**
A guideline/policy states that a control should exist.

**B. Implementation evidence**
Official evidence shows that the control has been deployed or used.

**C. Operational evidence**
Evidence shows the control is functioning consistently.

**D. Effectiveness evidence**
Evidence demonstrates that the control improves the relevant governance or outcome measure.

A government announcement or guideline should not be treated as D-level effectiveness evidence.

## What the CAG evidence supports

The evidence supports these research propositions:

1. PMKVY's digital systems generated large volumes of administrative information, but data completeness, validity, retention and traceability were not consistently assured.
2. Beneficiary eligibility rules were not consistently translated into operational system-based verification.
3. Monitoring reliability depended on evidence-validation controls that were incomplete or weak in several audited contexts.
4. Centre-State information access and integration affected the ability of some State bodies to perform monitoring.
5. Feedback and outcome tracking were important but historically limited by data and participation constraints.
6. Later PMKVY 4.0/SIDH architecture contains mechanisms that are relevant responses to several audit-identified control areas.
7. The existence of later controls does not by itself establish that the earlier weaknesses were fully resolved or that employment outcomes improved because of digitisation.

## What the evidence does NOT support

The final article should not claim:

- that PMKVY was wholly unsuccessful;
- that data-quality anomalies prove fraud or fake beneficiaries;
- that every underage/underqualified record represents intentional violation;
- that every questionable inspection was fabricated;
- that low AEBAS compliance proves non-attendance;
- that placement rates were caused by digital-governance weaknesses;
- that PMKVY 4.0/SIDH have already solved all earlier problems;
- that government-reported platform scale proves effectiveness.

## Proposed Control-to-Outcome Framework

### Stage 1 — Capture
Collect identity, eligibility, training, attendance, assessment and payment information.

### Stage 2 — Validate
Apply mandatory-field checks, format validation, de-duplication, age/qualification rules and other eligibility checks.

### Stage 3 — Trace
Retain original evidence and connect it to candidate, batch, centre, assessor/trainer and transaction records.

### Stage 4 — Monitor
Use authenticated users, geo/time validation, biometric attendance and risk-based inspection mechanisms.

### Stage 5 — Integrate
Provide authorised Centre and State bodies with timely, consistent and interoperable information.

### Stage 6 — Learn
Collect beneficiary feedback and post-certification outcome information.

### Stage 7 — Correct
Use validated evidence and outcome information for oversight, corrective action, recovery, redesign and programme improvement.

## Research contribution

The defensible contribution of the study is not to claim discovery of a new PMKVY failure. It is to provide an integrated **audit-based digital-governance interpretation** in which CAG observations are mapped across the complete information-control chain.

The contribution can be expressed as:

> The study organises dispersed PMKVY audit observations into a control-to-outcome governance chain linking data reliability, eligibility verification, evidence traceability, monitoring, interoperability, feedback and outcome tracking, and then examines subsequent PMKVY 4.0/SIDH mechanisms against this chain.

## Final P5 Synthesis

P5 establishes the empirical foundation for the next stage.

The central analytical proposition is:

> **The reliability of a digitally administered skill-development programme depends not merely on the existence of a digital platform, but on the connected operation of data-quality, verification, evidence-retention, monitoring, interoperability, feedback and outcome-tracking controls.**

This proposition is supported by the pattern of CAG findings but should be presented as the researcher's synthesis, not as a quotation or formal conclusion of CAG.
