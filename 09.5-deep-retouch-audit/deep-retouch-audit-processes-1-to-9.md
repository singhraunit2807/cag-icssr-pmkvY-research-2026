# Deep Retouch Audit — Processes 1–9

## Status
COMPLETE

## Purpose

A source-level review was performed across the research concept, literature review, research gap, methodology, CAG evidence analysis, reform comparison, results architecture, discussion framework and article architecture.

The review used the current official CAG report, current CAG–ICSSR guidelines, official PMKVY 4.0 guidelines, current official SIDH/PIB material and verified academic metadata.

## Major corrections identified

### 1. Competition structure corrected

The official 2026 CAG–ICSSR guidelines explicitly require:

- Introduction
- Conceptual and theoretical background
- Statement of the problem
- Objectives
- Methodology covering methodological approach, variables and tools employed
- Data analysis and findings
- Discussion
- Conclusions
- Policy Suggestions and the Way Forward

They also specify 4,000–5,000 words for UG/PG entries, MLA 9th edition, Times New Roman 12-point, standard margins and double spacing. Blind review is required and author-identifying information must not appear.

The previous P9 architecture was therefore retouched to mirror these headings explicitly.

### 2. AI-rule wording corrected

The current four-page official CAG–ICSSR 2026 guidelines reviewed here do not state an explicit AI-use prohibition in their listed submission conditions.

Therefore, an earlier internal statement that the official competition guidelines explicitly prohibit AI use should NOT be repeated as a verified competition rule.

The article must nevertheless remain the researcher's own scholarly work, and no author-identifying information should appear because the competition uses blind review.

### 3. Methodology corrected

P4 previously described analytical dimensions but did not explicitly label them as the operational “variables/dimensions” requested by the competition guidelines.

The retouched methodology now states that, because this is not a causal statistical study, the variables are operational analytical dimensions:

- data reliability;
- beneficiary verification;
- evidence retention;
- monitoring/system validation;
- interoperability/information access;
- feedback/outcome tracking;
- governance use.

The tools are explicitly identified as evidence matrix, spreadsheet calculations/denominator checks, thematic coding matrix, source-verification log and MLA 9 citation control.

### 4. CAG audit scope confirmed

The official CAG report states that the Performance Audit covers the first three PMKVY phases during 2015–22 and was conducted at MSDE, NSDC and eight selected States: Assam, Bihar, Jharkhand, Kerala, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh.

The report also contains later responses and updated PMKVY 4.0 observations. Therefore:

**2015–22 audit evidence ≠ later PMKVY 4.0 evidence**

The article must preserve this distinction.

### 5. Bank-account figure verified

Official CAG:

- 90,66,264 of 95,90,801 PMKVY 2.0/3.0 participant records had zero, Null, N/A or blank bank-account-detail values.
- CAG reports this as 94.53%.
- 12,122 unique bank-account numbers were repeated for 52,381 participants in the remaining records.

These figures are safe for the Results section when the denominator is stated.

### 6. Eligibility figures verified

Official CAG confirms:

- 6.54 lakh certified candidates across 705 job roles had not attained the minimum entry age according to the audit's comparison with the QP Master File.
- 1,142 candidates below age 18 were trained/certified in Driver/Chauffeur-related job roles where the QP required minimum age 18 and a valid licence.
- 6,77,807 of 60,68,523 candidates in job roles requiring education above Class 9 lacked recorded requisite information (11.17%).
- 8,09,046 of the same 60,68,523 candidates did not fulfil the required minimum educational qualification (13.33%).
- For prior technical qualification roles, 1,05,493 of 1,23,533 candidates (85.40%) were uneducated/basic literacy/regular education etc., while information was not recorded for 14,122 candidates (11.43%).
- The CAG also found 40,897 candidates certified in roles requiring formal education from Class 9 onward without the required minimum entry-level qualification and 4,361 candidates in roles requiring prior technical education without the requisite qualifications in its updated PMKVY data analysis.

The QP-version limitation must be stated: for PMKVY 1.0 and about 60.95 lakh PMKVY 2.0/3.0 certified candidates, the version of the QP/job role under which training was actually imparted was not recorded; the latest available QP Master File was therefore used for comparison.

### 7. Work-experience finding strengthened

The official CAG report states that the SIP data model had provision to record work experience, but the field was not being recorded for relevant job roles, so the method for ensuring compliance with the criterion had not been brought into effect.

Importantly, CAG further states that when updated PMKVY data were examined in October 2024, the provision for recording previous work experience was still not put in place in PMKVY 4.0.

This is a strong evidence-backed example of:

**system-field provision ≠ operational verification control**

### 8. Assessment-delay figures verified

Official CAG confirms:

- 48.53 lakh candidates in 1.61 lakh batches across the eight selected States were involved in the assessment analysis.
- Assessment delays after training completion reached 1,270 days.
- Final-certification delays beyond seven days reached 1,257 days.
- 82,158 batches (50.95%) involving 23.87 lakh candidates (49.20%) had such certification delays.

These figures are safe for use with the specified eight-State scope.

### 9. Feedback and Centre-State integration verified

Official CAG confirms:

- six of eight audited States lacked access to central-component training data from NSDC;
- this limited their monitoring ability;
- three States — Assam, Jharkhand and Odisha — reported that State/District Skill Monitoring Committees had never been formed;
- only 73,100 trainees, less than one per cent of certified candidates, had submitted feedback across all three phases as of September 2022.

These are particularly useful for the information-usability and feedback-loop dimensions.

### 10. Placement table verified

CAG reports for STT/SP certified candidates:

- PMKVY 1.0: 13.32 lakh certified, 2.23 lakh placed, 16.74%;
- PMKVY 2.0: 40.37 lakh certified, 20.62 lakh placed, 51.08%;
- PMKVY 3.0: 2.45 lakh certified, 0.33 lakh placed, 13.47%;
- total: 56.14 lakh certified, 23.18 lakh placed, 41.29%.

These are descriptive outcome figures and must not be interpreted as evidence that digital-governance weaknesses caused the placement rates.

### 11. Later PMKVY 4.0 evidence clarified

Official PMKVY 4.0 guidelines provide:

- mobile-based OTP/e-KYC profile verification;
- candidate de-duplication;
- candidate profiling using other portals;
- physical inspection through monitoring applications;
- assessment monitoring;
- one-year post-certification tracking for STT/SP candidates;
- quarterly tracking calls over one year;
- penalty provisions.

Later official MSDE material also documents virtual verification, call validation, CCTV monitoring and structured trainee feedback.

Therefore the reform layer contains meaningful control-design evidence.

However:

**design evidence ≠ operational effectiveness evidence**

### 12. SIDH current evidence verified

A July 2026 PIB release states that SIDH was launched on 13 September 2023 and describes it as a unified/interoperable digital platform connecting training, assessment, certification, employment and entrepreneurship services with government platforms including NCS, e-Shram, DigiLocker and BHASHINI.

The same release reports 35,17,023 learners registered under PMKVY 4.0 as per SIDH data.

These are government-reported implementation/scale figures, not independent effectiveness evidence.

### 13. Enforcement evidence verified

A 29 July 2026 PIB release states that under PMKVY 4.0:

- ₹2,314.09 crore was utilised during FY 2023–24 to FY 2025–26;
- payments exceeding ₹400 crore were withheld for further scrutiny due to trainer-attendance non-compliance;
- government also reports Aadhaar-based e-KYC, face authentication, geo-tagged attendance, QR-coded certificates, real-time SIDH dashboards, strengthened accreditation, independent monitoring, revised KPIs and penalty/recovery mechanisms.

This is evidence of reported implementation/enforcement. It does not prove overall effectiveness.

- fraud from data anomalies;
- fake beneficiaries from repeated identifiers;
- deliberate fabrication from monitoring anomalies;
- programme failure from selected audit findings;
- causal digital-governance effects on employment;
