# Process 2 — Literature Review & Verification

## Status
100% COMPLETE

## Purpose
To establish the verified academic, government and institutional literature relevant to PMKVY, public-sector data governance, digital government, data quality, interoperability and monitoring, and to identify a defensible research gap.

## Evidence hierarchy
1. CAG Report No. 20 of 2025 — primary audit evidence.
2. Official Government of India sources — MSDE/PMKVY/SIDH/DMEO/NITI Aayog/PIB.
3. Peer-reviewed academic literature.
4. OECD and other authoritative institutional sources.
5. Secondary sources only as leads, not as core evidence.

## Verified literature themes
### PMKVY-specific literature
The reviewed literature includes studies on programme impact, training effectiveness, implementation challenges, beneficiary experience and employment/employability outcomes. Therefore the research does NOT claim that PMKVY has never been studied.

### Digital-government/data-governance literature
Relevant verified literature covers:
- government data governance
- data management and data quality
- interoperability
- identity/data sharing
- digital-government assessment
- public-sector data collaboration

Important verified sources include:
- Mao, Wu, Qiao & Yao, “Government data governance framework based on a data middle platform,” Aslib Journal of Information Management 74(2), 289–310, DOI 10.1108/AJIM-03-2021-0068.
- Otjacques, Hitzelberger & Feltz, “Interoperability of E-Government Information Systems,” Journal of Management Information Systems.
- Margariti et al., “A holistic model for assessing organizational interoperability in public administration,” Government Information Quarterly.
- Schmeling, al Dakruni & Mergel, 2025 literature review on data collaboration in digital government.
- OECD, The Path to Becoming a Data-Driven Public Sector.
- DMEO/NITI Aayog Data Governance Quality Index (DGQI).

## Defensible research gap
The reviewed literature contains PMKVY-specific studies on programme impact, training effectiveness, implementation challenges and employment-related outcomes, while broader digital-government literature provides frameworks for data governance, data management and interoperability. However, within the literature reviewed for this study, there is limited integrated analysis of PMKVY audit evidence that brings together data quality, beneficiary verification, information retention, monitoring controls, system validation, Centre-State data integration and outcome tracking as interconnected governance functions.

## Important exclusions/corrections
- Do not claim that no research exists on PMKVY.
- Do not claim that digital governance has not been studied.
- Do not use an unverified/unreviewed preprint as core literature when stronger sources are available.
- Do not manufacture a research gap.
- Do not treat a government framework such as DGQI as PMKVY-specific.
