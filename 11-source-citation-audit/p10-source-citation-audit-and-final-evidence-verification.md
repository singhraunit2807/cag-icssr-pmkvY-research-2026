## Purpose

This process performs the final pre-drafting evidence audit for the research article. The objective is to ensure that every major empirical claim is:

**Claim → Primary/authoritative source → Exact evidence → Period → Population/scope → Interpretation boundary → Citation**

The audit prioritises the official CAG report and official CAG–ICSSR guidelines. Later policy/reform claims are checked against official MSDE/PIB sources. Academic sources are checked for bibliographic reliability and relevance.

# 3. Core CAG evidence matrix

## 3.1 Programme scale

### Verified
During the first three phases:
- outlay: approximately ₹14,450 crore;
- combined target: 1.32 crore candidates;
- 1.10 crore candidates certified;
- ₹10,194 crore released;
- ₹9,261 crore utilised.

### Interpretation boundary
These are programme-scale figures. They do not by themselves establish programme effectiveness.

---

## 3.2 Data retention and governance

### Verified
CAG found absence of a policy for retention of crucial electronic data/information. Information relating to participant attendance, trainer/assessor identity/contact details and training-centre/assessment geographic information for PMKVY 1.0 was not retained.

RPL photographic/video evidence was not retained at NSDC and was available lower in the implementation hierarchy.

CAG also noted that the Data Governance Framework was notified in April 2022, after completion of the three phases, and found that it did not adequately address important issues such as data ownership, security and management in the manner discussed by audit.

### Interpretation
Safe conclusion:

**The audit identified weaknesses in evidence retention, information governance and traceability.**

Do not conclude that all PMKVY data were lost or that records were intentionally deleted.

---

## 3.3 Trainer/assessor identity controls

### Verified
From 3.39 lakh PMKVY 2.0/3.0 batches:

- Trainer ID was Null/0 in 2,30,758 batches involving 61,12,030 certified candidates.
- Assessor contact number was Null/None in 3,27,220 batches involving 93,01,810 certified candidates.
- Assessor ID was 0.00 in 46 batches involving 874 candidates.

CAG stated that non-recording of identification/contact details did not provide assurance regarding completion of essential steps leading to certification.

### Interpretation
Safe analytical formulation:

**The evidence indicates weakened traceability of trainer and assessor participation within the digital record.**

Do not call these records fraudulent.

---

## 3.4 Bank-account data

### Verified
PMKVY 2.0/3.0:

- 90,66,264 of 95,90,801 participant records had zero, Null, N/A or blank bank-account-detail values.
- CAG reported this as 94.53%.
- Among the remaining records, 12,122 unique bank-account numbers were repeated for 52,381 participants.
- CAG also observed apparent invalid/account-placeholder values.

### Interpretation
CAG concluded that the account-number field did not provide adequate assurance about participant identity.

### Important boundary
Do not say:

- 94.53% beneficiaries had fake accounts;
- 94.53% beneficiaries were fake;
- repeated accounts prove fraud.

Correct formulation:

**94.53% of the examined PMKVY 2.0/3.0 participant records contained zero, Null, N/A or blank values in the bank-account-detail field.**

---

## 3.5 Contact-data quality

### Verified
CAG recorded:

- 179,407 unique email addresses associated with 7,120,995 candidates in repeated-use analysis;
- 390,508 unique mobile numbers associated with 1,056,475 candidates in repeated-use analysis;
- about 87,000 mobile numbers with fewer than 10 digits or apparent dummy patterns;
- “Migrated data” recorded in Candidate_Email for 36,49,344 candidates.

Beneficiary survey:
- 4,330 beneficiaries contacted;
- 36.51% emails failed;
- only 171 candidates responded, 3.95%;
- 131 of 171 responses (76.61%) were from the same email ID or TP/TC-linked email IDs.

### Interpretation
This is strong evidence for:

**contactability and electronic-identity reliability limitations.**

Do not describe the survey as representative of all beneficiaries.

# 4. Beneficiary verification evidence

## 4.1 Age verification

### Verified
CAG's comparison with the QP Master File found:

- 6.54 lakh certified candidates across 705 job roles had not attained the minimum entry age at the time of batch start.
- Example: 52,214 candidates in Group Farming Practitioner and 40,953 in Self Employed Tailor were below the respective QP minimum ages.
- Driver/Chauffeur-related job roles provided an example involving under-18 candidates.

### Critical limitation
CAG states that the version of the QP/job role under which training was actually imparted was not recorded for PMKVY 1.0 and about 60.95 lakh certified PMKVY 2.0/3.0 candidates. Therefore, the latest available QP Master File was used for comparison.

### Final interpretation
The article must say:

**“CAG's comparison with the latest available QP Master File identified…”**

rather than presenting every case as an unquestionable contemporaneous eligibility violation.

---

## 4.2 Education verification

### Verified
Among 60,68,523 candidates certified in job roles requiring education above Class 9:

- requisite information was not recorded for 6,77,807 candidates (11.17%);
- 8,09,046 candidates (13.33%) did not fulfil the required minimum educational qualification.

For job roles requiring prior technical qualification:

- 1,05,493 of 1,23,533 candidates (85.40%) were classified as uneducated/basic literacy/regular education etc.;
- information was not recorded for 14,122 candidates (11.43%).

### Interpretation
These findings establish deficiencies in recorded qualification information and CAG-identified non-conformity with the comparison criteria.

Do not infer intentional misrepresentation.

---

## 4.3 Work-experience verification

### Verified
CAG examined job roles requiring prior work experience and found that although the SIP data model had provision to record work experience, it was not being recorded. Therefore, the mechanism for ensuring compliance with the criterion had not been brought into effect.

CAG also reported that, in updated PMKVY 4.0 data examined in October 2024, the provision for recording previous work experience was still not put in place.

### Analytical importance
This is a strong evidence-backed illustration of:

**system-field availability ≠ operational verification control**

---

## 4.4 PMKVY 4.0 age/data controls — important later evidence

CAG's October 2024 examination of PMKVY 4.0 found:

- 1.18 lakh underage candidates across 365 job roles;
- 175 invalid mobile numbers;
- 2,263 repeated mobile numbers;
- more than 2.72 lakh null email addresses;
- more than 3.08 lakh repeated email addresses.

CAG stated that the requisite IT controls assured by the Ministry had yet to come into effect at the time examined.

### Interpretation
This is particularly important because it prevents an overly simple narrative:

**Historical weakness → new digital reform → problem solved**

The evidence instead supports:

**Historical weakness → new controls introduced/assured → residual weaknesses still observed in October 2024 → later reform evidence must be assessed separately.**

# 5. Assessment and certification controls

## Verified
In eight selected States:

- 48.53 lakh candidates;
- 1.61 lakh batches;
- assessment delays up to 1,270 days after training completion;
- final-certification delays beyond seven days up to 1,257 days;
- 82,158 batches (50.95%);
- 23.87 lakh candidates (49.20%).

### Interpretation
These figures support analysis of timeliness and control-process reliability.

Do not claim that every delay caused harm to candidates or employment outcomes unless separately evidenced.

# 6. Monitoring and evidence validation

## Verified

CAG scrutinised 1,463 inspection reports involving 14 skill sectors in the RPL-BICE monitoring analysis.

CAG found:

- 80 instances where multiple locations in different States were reported as physically visited by the same NSDC inspector(s) on the same day;
- deficiencies in photographic/inspection evidence;
- concerns about the reliability of monitoring evidence;
- use of geo-tagged inspection processes whose evidentiary purpose was weakened by shared login/upload practices.

The Ministry later reported creation of separate logins and restrictions intended to support real-time geo-tagged image uploads.

### Interpretation
The correct analytical concept is:

**monitoring signal → validation → exception → corrective action**

not merely:

**inspection count = effective monitoring**

Do not use words such as “fabricated inspections” unless directly supported by the specific evidence.

---

# 7. AEBAS evidence

### Verified
CAG states:

- PMKVY 2.0 guidelines prescribed AEBAS;
- Steering Committee mandated AEBAS from 1 April 2018;
- AEBAS was not followed in RPL projects, which represented around 50% of total PMKVY training;
- 24 Training Centres had biometric devices not installed or not working;
- as of January 2025, 85% of candidates were registered on AEBAS;
- CAG stated AEBAS was still not fully implemented in PMKVY 4.0.

### Interpretation
Do not say:

**“AEBAS failure proves candidates did not attend.”**

Correct:

**“The audit identified incomplete implementation of the prescribed biometric attendance control.”**

---

# 8. Centre-State integration

### Verified
CAG identified variations between Skill India Portal and State Skill Development Office data and referred to:

- API integration issues;
- delays in pushing data;
- data-access issues.

Six of the eight selected States lacked access to central-component training data from NSDC, limiting their monitoring ability.

### Interpretation
This supports analysis of:

**technical/data integration → administrative access → monitoring usability**

Do not conclude that all State systems were technically incompatible.

---

# 9. State/District monitoring committees

### Verified
CAG reported:

- Assam, Jharkhand and Odisha stated that State/District Skill Monitoring Committees had never been formed.
- Other States provided different responses.
- Maharashtra had constituted and restructured State/District committees.

### Interpretation
These are selected-State findings and must not be generalised to all States.

---

# 10. Feedback

### Verified
As of September 2022:

- only 73,100 trainees, less than one per cent of certified candidates, had submitted feedback across all three phases.

CAG stated that reliance on alternative methods instead of mandated feedback collection weakened the feedback mechanism.

### Interpretation
This supports the argument that:

**feedback collection → evidence generation → course correction**

was not functioning at the intended scale.

Do not claim that beneficiaries universally lacked satisfaction or that feedback was universally negative.

| PMKVY 3.0 | 2.45 | 0.33 | 13.47% |

### Important CAG context
CAG itself discussed low placement alongside training in disregard of QP minimum criteria and absence of strategy/plan, and noted that the Ministry had identified job-role selection without skill-gap/market-demand analysis as a primary reason for poor placement.

### Research boundary
The article must **not** convert these observations into a causal claim that digital-governance weaknesses caused placement rates.

The placement table is descriptive outcome evidence that demonstrates why outcome tracking matters, not proof of a digital-governance causal effect.

# 14. Evidence maturity model — LOCKED

Every later reform will be classified as:

### A — Design
Guideline, policy, specification or announced mechanism.

### B — Implementation
Evidence that the mechanism has been deployed or used.

### C — Operational
Evidence that it functions consistently and produces reliable control information.

### D — Effectiveness
Credible evidence demonstrating improved reliability, verification, monitoring, feedback or outcomes attributable to the mechanism.

## Current evidence position

PMKVY 4.0/SIDH evidence provides substantial **A-level** evidence and selected **B-level** evidence.

Some enforcement actions also support B-level implementation evidence.

The current evidence reviewed does not justify automatically classifying all reforms as C or D.

# 17. Claims requiring careful wording

## Instead of:
“PMKVY had fake beneficiaries.”

Use:
“CAG identified weaknesses in beneficiary identity and eligibility verification, including incomplete and repeated electronic identifiers.”

## Instead of:
“PMKVY's digital system failed.”

Use:
“The audit identified weaknesses in the reliability, retention, validation and use of information generated through the digital implementation system.”

## Instead of:
“PMKVY 4.0 fixed the problems.”

Use:
“PMKVY 4.0 introduced or strengthened controls corresponding to several audit-identified weaknesses; the available evidence does not establish that all weaknesses were eliminated.”

## Instead of:
“Digital governance caused low placement.”

Use:
“The placement figures provide descriptive outcome evidence, while the audit identified multiple planning, eligibility and implementation issues. The available evidence does not establish a causal effect of digital-governance weaknesses on placement.”
