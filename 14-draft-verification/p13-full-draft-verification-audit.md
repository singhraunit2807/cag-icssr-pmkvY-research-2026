# Process 13 — Full Draft Verification Audit

## Status
100% COMPLETE

## Result
The P12 first full article draft passed the substantive evidence audit after targeted corrections.

## 1. Word-count audit
The article body is approximately 4,800 words, within the official UG/PG limit of 4,000–5,000 words. The raw GitHub file count is higher because it contains process metadata and audit notes outside the article body.

## 2. Competition-structure audit
The official CAG–ICSSR guidelines require Introduction; Conceptual and Theoretical Background; Statement of the Problem; Objectives; Methodology covering methodological approach, variables and tools; Data Analysis and Findings; Discussion; Conclusions; and Policy Suggestions and the Way Forward. The draft contains all required substantive sections and remains within the prescribed word range.

## 3. Primary CAG audit
The article correctly separates the main audit period (2015–22) from later reform/update evidence. It also correctly preserves the selected eight-State scope.

A critical scope correction was made: CAG reviewed PMKVY 4.0 guideline changes in August–October 2024, but explicitly states that implementation of PMKVY 4.0 according to those guidelines was not examined. The article now says this directly.

## 4. Numerical claims verified
The following major numerical claims are supported by the official CAG report:
- approximately ₹14,450 crore outlay, 1.32 crore target, 1.10 crore certified, ₹10,194 crore released and ₹9,261 crore utilised;
- 90,66,264 of 95,90,801 bank-account fields, or 94.53%, containing zero, Null, N/A or blank values;
- 12,122 repeated unique bank accounts used for 52,381 participants;
- Trainer ID Null/0 in 2,30,758 batches involving 61,12,030 certified candidates;
- Assessor contact Null/None in 3,27,220 batches involving 93,01,810 certified candidates;
- 36.51% email delivery failure in the audit beneficiary-contact exercise, 171 responses from 4,330 contacted candidates, and 131 responses linked to the same or TP/TC-related email IDs;
- 6.54 lakh candidates across 705 job roles below the minimum age used in the CAG comparison;
- 6,77,807 records without required education information and 8,09,046 not meeting the minimum educational qualification identified by the audit;
- assessment delays up to 1,270 days;
- certification delays up to 1,257 days in 82,158 batches involving 23.87 lakh candidates;
- six of eight audited States lacking access to central-component training data from NSDC;
- 73,100 trainee feedback submissions, less than one per cent of certified candidates, as of September 2022;
- 56.14 lakh STT/SP certified and 23.18 lakh placed, with 41.29% overall placement.

## 5. Interpretation audit
The article does not treat repeated accounts, incomplete identifiers or monitoring anomalies as proof of fraud or intentional manipulation. It does not treat AEBAS gaps as proof of non-attendance. It does not claim that digital-governance weaknesses caused placement outcomes. It does not claim that PMKVY 4.0 or SIDH solved all earlier weaknesses.

## 6. QP-version limitation
The article correctly states that the actual QP/job-role version under which training was imparted was not recorded for PMKVY 1.0 and about 60.95 lakh certified PMKVY 2.0/3.0 candidates. CAG therefore used the latest available QP Master File for comparison. The 6.54 lakh age finding is presented with this limitation.

## 7. Data-retention audit
The article's retention discussion is supported by CAG's finding that the Ministry had not formulated an adequate electronic-record retention policy, resulting in non-retention of attendance, trainer/assessor identity/contact and geographic information for PMKVY 1.0. CAG also found that RPL photographs/videos were not retained at NSDC and that incomplete evidence disrupted the transaction trail.

## 8. AEBAS audit
The article's AEBAS section is supported by CAG's finding that, among 98,488 PMKVY 2.0/3.0 STT/SP batches involving 23,64,359 participants, only 12,538 batches involving 3,09,615 participants were AEBAS compliant, approximately 13%. CAG also found that 24 of 86 surveyed Training Centres lacked the required AEBAS infrastructure.

## 9. Centre-State and feedback audit
CAG confirms that six of eight audited States lacked access to central-component training data from NSDC. CAG also reports that only 73,100 trainees had submitted mandated feedback by September 2022. The article correctly treats these as information-access and feedback-control issues rather than evidence of beneficiary dissatisfaction.

## 10. Academic literature audit
The four core academic sources have verified bibliographic metadata:
- Mao, Wu, Qiao, and Yao, *Aslib Journal of Information Management*, 74(2), 2022, 289–310, DOI 10.1108/AJIM-03-2021-0068.
- Otjacques, Hitzelberger, and Feltz, *Journal of Management Information Systems*, 23(4), 2007, 29–51, DOI 10.2753/MIS0742-1222230403.
- Margariti et al., *Government Information Quarterly*, 39(3), 2022, article 101712, DOI 10.1016/j.giq.2022.101712.
- Schmeling, al Dakruni, and Mergel, *Government Information Quarterly*, 42(3), 2025, article 102063, DOI 10.1016/j.giq.2025.102063.

They are used for conceptual/theoretical claims, not for PMKVY empirical figures.

- e-KYC/Aadhaar-based authentication;
- mobile authentication;
- physical inspection;
- assessment monitoring;
- one-year post-certification tracking.

The official PMKVY 4.0 guideline has been added to Works Cited.

## 12. MLA audit
The draft now has:
- parenthetical citations after major source-derived claims;
- DOI information for academic articles;
- government guideline entry in Works Cited.

Final formatting still needs to be applied to the submission copy: Times New Roman 12, double spacing, standard margins, consistent headings and final page/table citation check.

## 13. Originality-risk audit
The draft uses the method:
source fact → paraphrase → researcher interpretation → governance implication.

Long CAG passages have not been reproduced. No claim of being the first study has been made. Similarity percentage cannot be guaranteed without the competition's actual checking system, but the draft is structured as original analytical prose rather than a reproduction of the audit report.

## 14. Claims deliberately excluded
The following remain excluded because they are not sufficiently verified for the locked evidence base:

- unverified NITI Aayog DGQI quantitative scores;
- unverified geo-tagging percentages;
